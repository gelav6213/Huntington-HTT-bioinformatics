2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?
- Knowing the exact location tells us where the variant is found within the gene. It helps us determine whether the variant is in an exon, intron, UTR, splice region, or another part of the gene. It also allows us to connect the variant reported in ClinVar with its actual position in the genome. This can help us better understand how the variant might affect the gene or its protein.

3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?
- One limitation is that the location alone cannot tell us exactly what effect the variant has on the gene or protein. Even if a variant is found in a coding exon, we cannot conclude its disease effect based only on its position. More evidence, such as functional and family studies, is needed to understand its actual effect. Therefore, genomic location can give us a clue, but it cannot prove that a variant causes disease.
